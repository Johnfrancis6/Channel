# Economics and monetization of US/English finance YouTube channels (2025-2026)

General caveat on sources (read first): almost every niche RPM/CPM figure published for 2025-2026 comes from SEO blogs run by tool vendors (OutlierKit, Lenostube, vidIQ-style calculators, sponsorship marketplaces). They do not disclose a methodology or sample, and they often contradict each other, even within a single page. YouTube publishes no RPM figures by niche. Treat every range below as an order of magnitude, not a measurement. Searches did not turn up any recent r/PartneredYoutube thread with a disclosed finance dashboard (gap).

## 1. CPM / RPM by finance sub-niche, seasonality, long-form vs Shorts

### Takeaway
For a US-majority long-form finance channel, a realistic planning RPM is about **$8–$15 per 1,000 views**, with $15–$25+ possible on "money-intent" topics such as credit cards, taxes, investing and retirement. Business documentaries and economics explainers sit lower, around **$5–$14**. Q4 can raise RPM by roughly 1.5–2x, and January drops sharply. Shorts pay roughly **100 to 300 times less** per view than long-form.

### Cited findings

- Aggregated search summary of 2026 blogs: finance RPM roughly $8–$35 depending on the source. One calculator gives $9–$11 RPM for a US audience, another $10–$25, another $12–$35. Investing CPM $20–$40, personal finance CPM $15–$25. The blogs themselves note that "no one publishes a measured RPM by niche". — [LearningRevolution calculator](https://www.learningrevolution.net/youtube-earnings-calculator/), [OutlierKit niches](https://outlierkit.com/blog/highest-paying-youtube-niches), [vloggingpro](https://vloggingpro.com/youtube-cpm/), [Miraflow](https://miraflow.ai/blog/youtube-cpm-rates-by-niche-2026-how-much-youtubers-earn), [ytmoneycalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/average-youtube-rpm/)

### Inferences
- The figure of $29.30 cited both by OutlierKit (Mayo) and in the creator roundup is probably the same data point, from a credit-card / personal finance creator in Q4. It is a ceiling, not an average.
- The sub-niches with the highest RPMs (credit cards, taxes, investing) are exactly the ones where advertisers sell high-value financial products. "Story" formats (business documentaries, billionaires) attract more general advertisers and pay less per view, but they reach a broader audience.
- For a planning model, $10 is a good central RPM for "investing/personal finance" and $7 for "business documentaries", with an annual average that smooths over Q4 and January.

### Gaps
- No measured, sourced RPM for "economics explainers", "wealth/luxury/billionaire stories" or "insurance" as a standalone niche.
- No direct Reddit dashboard disclosure found (the searches surfaced only blogs).
- The OutlierKit figures (the most detailed) have no methodology and the publisher is a commercial vendor.

## 2. YouTube Partner Program: thresholds, Shorts, time to monetization

### Takeaway
Until January 31, 2027, ad revenue requires **1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours in 12 months, or 10M Shorts views in 90 days**. **From February 1, 2027, new applicants will need 8,000 hours or 20M Shorts views**, with 1,000 subscribers unchanged. Channels already in YPP keep their status. A channel launched in November 2026 will almost certainly face the new threshold.

### Cited findings
- Current threshold: 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 valid public long-form watch hours (12 months) or 10M valid public Shorts views (90 days). The two paths do not combine: Shorts-feed views do not count toward watch hours. — [vidIQ, 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/monetize-youtube-guide/), [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)
- Lower "fan funding" tier: 500 subscribers + 3 uploads in 90 days + 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views. It unlocks memberships, Super Chat, Shopping and Creator Partnerships, but **not** ad revenue. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/monetize-youtube-guide/), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/new-youtube-creators-face-8-000-hour-bar-for-ad-revenue-from-february-2027/)
- **2027 change** announced August 10, 2026 (Team YouTube community post + Help Center), reported August 12, 2026. From February 1, 2027: 1,000 subscribers + **8,000 "qualified" hours** in 365 days or **20M "qualified" Shorts views** in 90 days. No cross-counting (example given: 5,000 h + 15M Shorts = not eligible). YouTube quote: "If you are already in YPP, your YPP status & ability to earn is not impacted by this update." New activity rule: 1,000 h/year, or 1M Shorts per 90 days, or 2 long-form videos / 5 Shorts per 90 days. Creators must accept the Watch Page / Shorts Monetization modules by January 31, 2027, or they stop earning on February 1, 2027. — [PPC Land, August 12, 2026](https://ppc.land/new-youtube-creators-face-8-000-hour-bar-for-ad-revenue-from-february-2027/), [vidIQ 2027 changes](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- Shorts watched as ads do not count toward the 20M threshold. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/shorts-watched-as-ads-do-not-count-toward-youtubes-20-million-view-bar/)
- Note: I did not verify the YouTube Help Center page directly. The secondary sources agree with one another.

### Inferences
- 8,000 hours = 480,000 minutes. With 12-minute videos watched for about 40% of their length (~5 min/view), that is **about 96,000 long-form views within 12 months** (versus ~48,000 under the old rule). With 20-minute videos at ~7 min/view, about 69,000 views. Longer videos (15–20 min) therefore help mechanically.
- A channel already in YPP before February 1, 2027 is grandfathered. Reaching 4,000 h + 1,000 subscribers in under 3 months is unlikely for a new channel launched in November 2026, so planning should assume 8,000 h.
- Shorts do not count toward hours, but they can bring subscribers (the 1,000 threshold).

### Gaps
- No reliable source on the **typical time to monetization for a finance channel** (the blogs cite 6–12 months without data). Not found.

## 3. Affiliate programs, sponsorships, disclosure rules

### Takeaway
Finance affiliates pay **per funded account or per approval** ($5–$200+ per conversion), which makes even a small channel profitable on targeted videos. But brokerage programs are **regulated by FINRA**, which supervises influencer content, and credit-card programs pay well but often require approval. Finance sponsorships run about **$25–$80 CPM** (some agencies say up to $200), so they are 2 to 6 times more lucrative per view than AdSense. FTC disclosure is mandatory and must be clear and in the video itself.

### Cited findings
**Brokers / investing**
- Webull (via Impact): $20–$70 per funded account, 30-day cookie, monthly payment according to an aggregator. Other sites say $100 per funded account or "$50–$200". Conflicting figures. — [CommissionDex 2026](https://commissiondex.com/programs/webull/), [BloggingTips](https://www.bloggingtips.com/affiliate-programs/webull), [magentheme](https://www.magentheme.com/webull-affiliate-program-review-pros-payouts/)
- Moomoo (FlexOffers): $40 per account with deposit, 30-day cookie. The page is about 618 days old (2024 data). — [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/moomoo-affiliate-program/)
- Robinhood (Impact): $5 per signup, $20 per funded account (aggregator, undated). — [Backlinko financial affiliate programs](https://backlinko.com/financial-affiliate-programs), [realize.com](https://realize.com/marketing-hub/financial-services-affiliate-programs/)
- SoFi (Impact): up to $150 per lead, $100–$150 per funded loan/account, $75 per checking/savings account, student loan refinancing up to $1,000. Cap of $10,000/year according to one site. Minimum payout about $50. Conflicting figures. — [getlasso 2026](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/sofi/), [UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/sofi/), [linkclicky 2024](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/sofi/)
- No figures found for Public, M1 (affiliate), TradingView, Seeking Alpha, tax software, budgeting apps or crypto exchanges (see Gaps).

**Credit cards**
- Bankrate (card affiliate program): $50–$175 per approval (CPA + revenue share) or "$50–$200". Market ranges: $25–$300+ per approved application, standard cards $20–80, secured cards $15–35, tiers that rise with volume (e.g. $100 per approval at 1–49 approvals). Approval depends on the creditworthiness of the audience. — [UpPromote credit card](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-programs/credit-card/), [afftank](https://afftank.com/blog/credit-card-affiliate-programs), [vellko 2026](https://vellko.com/blog/best-credit-card-affiliate-programs-2026/), [wecantrack](https://wecantrack.com/insights/credit-card-affiliate-programs/)
- Nothing found specifically on CardRatings or on minimum eligibility criteria for small channels.

**Regulatory framework for brokers (FINRA)**
- March 2024: FINRA fined M1 Finance **$850,000**, its first action against a firm over its influencer program. About 1,700 influencers between 2020 and 2023 and nearly 40,000 accounts opened, with a **flat fee per new customer** via referral links. M1 had not reviewed or approved the content. Some posts were "exaggerated, unwarranted, promissory or misleading" (Rules 2210(d)(1) and 2010), for example claims about margin. — [Fortune, March 19, 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/03/19/m1-finance-finra-sec-enforcement-fine-social-media-influencers), [ThinkAdvisor, March 18, 2024](https://thinkadvisor.com/2024/03/18/finra-levies-first-enforcement-action-in-finfluencers-sweep), [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/43870/finra-fines-m1-finance-over-misleading-social-media-influencer-posts/retail)
- Cobra Trading: $200,000 fine for failing to supervise its influencers' ads. — [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/regulation-compliance/finra-fines-cobra-trading-for-not-supervising-influencers-ads)
- FINRA found more than 29,000 accounts opened and funded through referral links given to influencers, with some posts misleading. — via search, [Wikipedia Moomoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moomoo_(company)) (context to confirm)
- Law firms advise brokers to supervise and archive influencer content. — [Troutman Pepper, May 22, 2024](https://www.troutman.com/insights/influencer-considerations-as-finra-initiates-crackdown.html)

**FTC disclosure**
- The Endorsement Guides were revised in 2023. Test: any material connection that would change how consumers weigh the recommendation must be disclosed, "clear and conspicuous", inside the content itself. YouTube's built-in "paid promotion" label alone may not be enough. Avoid vague tags (#sp, #ambassador). — [Traverse Legal](https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/ftc-influencer-guidelines/), [Revision Legal](https://revisionlegal.com/internet-law/youtubers-ftc-endorsement-rules-compliance/), [Kajabi help](https://help.kajabi.com/en/articles/12696554-ftc-guidelines)
- Civil penalties of up to $50,120 per violation were cited in FTC warning letters to 12 influencers (inflation-adjusted amount, possibly outdated). — [Keller and Heckman](https://www.khlaw.com/index%2ephp/insights/ftc-warns-two-trade-associations-and-12-online-influencers-better-disclose-financial)
- Warning: claims of a "dual-disclosure obligation from March 13, 2026" and "$265,440 in fines for 5 videos" (DEV Community) match no official source. Do not rely on them. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/tryspansa/youtube-ftc-disclosure-rules-2026-brand-co-liability-31i2)

**Finance sponsorships**
- Finance sponsorship CPM: $25–$65 according to one source, $40–$80 according to another ("Finance & business"), and $50–$200 according to an agency for long-form personal finance / investing / real estate / taxes. Example: 100,000 average views × $40 CPM = about $4,000 per integration. — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/), [OutlierKit sponsorship](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/), [CreatorsAgency finance vs lifestyle](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates-finance-vs-lifestyle), [SponsorRadar $10–80 CPM](https://sponsorradar.com/insights/youtube-sponsorship-rates-what-brands-should-pay)
- Geography: a finance channel with a 70% US audience gets $55–$80 CPM. The same channel with a 70% India/Pakistan audience gets $10–$20. — via search, [collabonly](https://blog.collabonly.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates)
- Rates reported on Reddit (r/NewTubers, r/PartneredYoutube) in 2026: about $15–$25 CPM for lifestyle and $30–$60 for tech/finance (secondhand report). — via search, [influenceflow](https://influenceflow.io/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates-2025-a-creators-guide-to-fair-pricing/)
- Claim that AdSense makes up only 30–40% of top finance creators' income, with sponsorships at 5–10x AdSense (self-reported, unverified). — [CreatorsAgency, finance creator income](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/finance-youtube-creator-income-breakdown)

### Inferences
- At equal views, a sponsorship at $40 CPM brings in about 4x an AdSense RPM of $10. Sponsorships become the main revenue line once a channel steadily reaches about 20–50k views per video. Business documentaries attract sponsors (ColdFusion and MagnatesMedia are cited for "strong sponsors").
- Affiliate links are available before YPP (no subscriber threshold for most Impact programs, subject to approval). They are the only real revenue lever during the 8,000-hour phase.
- Broker referral links make the creator's content subject to the broker's FINRA review. Promising returns or minimizing risks (margin, options) is exactly what got M1 fined. "Story" formats carry less of this exposure.

### Gaps
- Public.com, M1 (current affiliate program), TradingView, Seeking Alpha, tax software (TurboTax/H&R Block), budgeting apps (Monarch, YNAB, Rocket Money), crypto exchanges (Coinbase, Kraken) and Skillshare/Audible-type sponsors: **no figures found** within the search budget. Check the official pages (Impact, CJ, partner pages).
- Minimum eligibility criteria for small channels (audience size, approval by networks) were not documented by any primary source.

## 4. Best sub-niche (RPM × audience × affiliate fit × competition) and saturation of business documentaries

### Takeaway
No sourced data allows a quantitative ranking. The available evidence suggests a trade-off: **credit cards / investing / taxes** offer the best RPM and affiliate fit but a narrow audience, high competition and regulatory risk. **Business documentaries / company breakdowns** offer a large audience and good watch time, but a lower RPM ($5–$14) and a faceless format that many guides push. Saturation is asserted, but no measured evidence was found.

### Cited findings
- Business documentaries: RPM $5–$14 (search volume estimated at "600K"), "strong sponsors plus evergreen views". Example channels: ColdFusion, MagnatesMedia, Company Man. — [OutlierKit faceless](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-channels/)
- One guide classifies "History & Documentary" as a "low saturation" niche, while the explicitly saturated niche is "Reddit stories" ($2–$5 RPM). — [reelpilot 2026](https://reelpilot.app/blog/faceless-youtube-channel-ideas-2026), [faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/how-much-do-faceless-youtube-channels-make/)
- Indirect sign of saturation: dozens of "faceless YouTube" guides and kits (Gumroad, AI tools) list business documentaries among the recommended niches. — [nexlev](https://www.nexlev.io/faceless-youtube-channel-profitable), [clippie](https://clippie.ai/blog/faceless-youtube-channels-make-money-2026), [virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/ai-faceless-youtube-channel-guide-2026)
- Credit cards: highest RPM ($16–$26 average) and highest per-approval affiliate commissions ($50–$175 at Bankrate). — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche), [UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-programs/credit-card/)

### Inferences
- "Saturation" of faceless business documentaries is mostly a perception driven by the volume of guides recommending them, not by any measurement. The "low saturation" verdict in the guides is just as unsubstantiated. Assessing it would require observing the competing channels (another researcher's scope).
- A hybrid path seems economically consistent: a "company stories / why X costs money" format (broad audience, high watch time) with a personal finance or investing angle (RPM pulled toward $10–$15, and natural slots for broker, budgeting or card affiliate links), while avoiding promises of returns (FINRA).
- Crypto: lower RPM ($9–$16) and higher regulatory/reputational risk. Not a priority on the numbers.

### Gaps
- No competition metric (number of channels, average views per new channel) by sub-niche found in any reliable source.
- No measured RPM for the "billionaires / luxury" niche.

## 5. Realistic revenue model (100k, 500k, 1M long-form views per month)

### Takeaway
On AdSense alone, with a US-majority audience: **100k views/month ≈ $500–$2,000; 500k ≈ $2,500–$10,000; 1M ≈ $5,000–$20,000** depending on sub-niche and season. Sponsorships and affiliates can double or triple that total in finance.

### Cited findings
- Finance long-form 10+ min RPM $13–$26, that is **$13,000–$26,000 per 1M views** (optimistic estimate). 8–10 min: $11,000–$21,000 per 1M. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche)
- Conservative calculator: finance US $9–$11 RPM, about $9,000–$11,000 per 1M views. — [LearningRevolution](https://www.learningrevolution.net/youtube-earnings-calculator/)
- Faceless channels: a "strong" niche channel makes $2,000–$15,000/month, a small channel $100–$500/month (unsourced estimate). — via search, [faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/how-much-do-faceless-youtube-channels-make/)
- Nischa (personal finance): $500–$9,700/month according to Social Blade (third-party estimate). — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche)
- Sponsorships: about $4,000 per integration for 100k average views at $40 CPM. — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)

### Inferences (model calculations, not sourced data)
Annual-average RPM assumptions: business documentaries $5–$10; personal finance/investing $8–$15; credit cards/taxes $15–$25.

| Long-form views/month | Business docs ($5–10) | Personal finance / investing ($8–15) | Cards / taxes ($15–25) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100k | $500–1,000 | $800–1,500 | $1,500–2,500 |
| 500k | $2,500–5,000 | $4,000–7,500 | $7,500–12,500 |
| 1M | $5,000–10,000 | $8,000–15,000 | $15,000–25,000 |

- Q4: multiply by about 1.5–2. January: about 0.5–0.7 (using OutlierKit's Q1 $8–15 vs Q4 $18–30, and Lenostube's January $2.99 vs December $5.70 CPM).
- Sponsorships: with 4 videos/month at a 1M total (250k views/video) and 1 to 2 sponsored videos at $30–$60 CPM, that is about $7,500–$30,000/month in addition. At 100k/month (25k views/video), sponsorship is possible but sporadic (about $750–$1,500 per integration).
- Affiliates (illustrative assumption): 1M views × 0.05–0.1% conversion to funded accounts × $30–$100 = $15,000–$100,000... that range is too wide to rely on. No conversion-rate data for YouTube was found, so treat this as a gap.
- Ramp-up for a new channel: no revenue before YPP (from February 2027, ~70–100k long-form views needed for 8,000 h). Only affiliates can produce income during this phase.
- A US-majority audience is the main multiplier. An English-speaking channel with a lot of Indian/Pakistani audience can see its sponsorship CPMs divided by 3 to 5 ([collabonly](https://blog.collabonly.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates)), and likewise its RPM.

### Gaps
- No audited dashboard for a finance channel at 100k/500k/1M monthly views in 2025-2026.
- No conversion rates for finance affiliate links on YouTube.
- No source on the typical time to reach YPP in finance.
