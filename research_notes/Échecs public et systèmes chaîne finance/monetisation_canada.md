# Monetizing an English finance YouTube channel from Canada (US-majority audience), as of November 2026

Scope: a creator who is a tax resident of Canada runs a faceless, English, long-form (8–20 min) finance channel. Most viewers are in the US, with some in Canada. This file builds on `research_notes/Croissance chaînes YouTube finance/economie_niche.md` (RPM by sub-niche, sponsor CPMs, FINRA M1 case, FTC) and does not repeat those figures except where needed.

Method note: research done on 2026-11-05. I checked the official Google pages through search excerpts (the YPP changes page, the YouTube Blog) and one direct fetch (the YouTube US tax help page). Most affiliate and tax-blog figures come from aggregators that disclose no methodology. Each line is labelled with its source type. **This is not legal or tax advice.** Points marked "VERIFY (accountant)" should be confirmed with a Canadian cross-border accountant.

## 1. YouTube Partner Program from Canada: eligibility, payments, US withholding, Canadian tax, 2027 threshold

### Takeaway
A Canadian resident can join YPP. **The move to 8,000 hours from 2027-02-01 is CONFIRMED by YouTube's own Help Center and Blog.** A channel launched in November 2026 will in practice have to reach 1,000 subscribers + 8,000 qualified hours (365 days), or 20M Shorts views (90 days). US withholding applies only to revenue from US viewers. With a treaty claim in the AdSense tax tool, the rate for a Canadian resident is **most often reported as 0%, but some sources say 10%**. Google does not name Canada's rate, and 15% is only a generic example on Google's page. Without tax info, Google withholds up to 24% of worldwide earnings. In Canada, YouTube income is business income. For GST/HST, AdSense revenue is generally treated as zero-rated, but it still counts toward the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold.

### Cited findings
**2027 YPP threshold: confirmed (official sources)**
- The YouTube Help Center page "Changes to the YouTube Partner Program" says: "Starting Feb. 1, 2027, YPP entry thresholds for new creators are changing to 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, in addition to still needing 1k subscribers." This applies to ad and Premium revenue sharing. "If you are already in YPP, your status is not impacted by this update." — [YouTube Help, answer 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en) (2026, seen via search excerpt)
- Fan funding, YouTube Creator Partnerships and YouTube Shopping thresholds do not change. They remain **500 subscribers + 3,000 qualified hours (12 months) or 3M Shorts views (90 days)**. — [YouTube Help, answer 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- From 2027-02-01, a channel counts as "active" if it has 1,000 qualified hours in 365 days, or 1M Shorts views in 90 days, or 2 long-form videos or 5 Shorts per 90 days. A channel below these levels gets a 90-day window to recover. Creators must accept the updated terms in Studio by 2027-01-31, or they stop earning from the affected features on 2027-02-01. — [YouTube Help, answer 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- The YouTube Blog announcement (2026-08-10) calls this "the first significant changes since 2018", for more than 3M creators in YPP. It also announces an expanded Premium Lite pool shared 55% long-form / 45% Shorts. — [YouTube Blog, "New opportunities to earn and changes to the YouTube Partner Program"](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)
- Hours earned in late 2026 count toward an application made after 2027-02-01, because the window looks back 365 days. — [relevantaudience (secondary)](https://www.relevantaudience.com/youtube/youtube-partner-program-thresholds-double-february-2027/)

**US withholding (Google, official)**
- With valid tax info, creators outside the US have "between 0–30%" withheld on earnings from US viewers, depending on whether their country has a treaty with the US. Without tax info, Google "may be required to withhold taxes worldwide using the maximum tax rate": **24% of worldwide earnings** for an Individual account, or 30% of US earnings for a Business account outside the US. The page's example uses a generic treaty rate of 15% against 30% without a treaty. It does not name Canada's rate. — [YouTube Help, "US tax requirements for YouTube earnings", answer 10391362](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362), fetched 2026-11-05 (no update date on the page)
- Revenue subject to withholding (US viewers only): ad views, YouTube Premium, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks, channel memberships. Treaty benefits can reduce withholding on "Other Copyright Royalties" (such as YPP) and on "Services" (such as AdSense). Submit the form and claim the treaty in the "AdSense for YouTube" tax tool, by **December 10 each year** for the current year. — [YouTube Help 10391362](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362)
- The W-8BEN goes to the platform, not to the IRS. A Canadian enters Canada as country of residence and their **SIN** as the foreign tax ID. W-8 forms expire at the end of the third calendar year after signing. — [Remitly (2025-26)](https://www.remitly.com/blog/en-ca/personal-finance/the-w8-ben-form-essential-guide), [Wise CA](https://wise.com/ca/blog/form-w-8ben-canada), [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/non-u-s-creators-24-percent-tax-youtube-revenue/)
- **Canada's treaty rate is disputed.** Several creator-tax guides say 0% for Canada. Others give 10% under Article XII, "0% in some cases". A Canadian on the Udemy forum says it is "not across the board 0's". — [fluxnote 2026](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-creator-taxes-canada-2026), [fluxnote W-8BEN 2026](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-tax-withholding-w8ben-2026), [taxfriend](https://taxfriend.pages.dev/profession-guides/blog-content-creators-influencers-tax-guide.html), [Udemy community](https://community.udemy.com/en/discussion/41211/tax-form-instructions-for-canadian-instructors), [acadifi](https://acadifi.com/community/how-to-determine-treaty-rate-w-8ben)
- Treaty mechanism (secondary sources: CRA interpretations). Article XII(3) exempts at source copyright royalties for literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works. The exemption excludes uses in connection with **television broadcasting**, while motion pictures and videotapes for private (home) use are exempt. Other royalties are capped at **10%**. — [CRA interpretation 2013-0475751E5 (via videotax)](https://members.videotax.com/technical-interpretations/2013-0475751E5-withholding-tax-on-royalty-payments), [IRS technical explanation (canatech.pdf)](https://www.stayexempt.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/canatech.pdf)
- US tax withheld can be claimed as a foreign tax credit on the Canadian T1 return. — [fluxnote 2026](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-creator-taxes-canada-2026), [Remitly](https://www.remitly.com/blog/en-ca/personal-finance/the-w8-ben-form-essential-guide) (secondary)

**GST/HST (Canada)**
- Small supplier: total worldwide taxable **and zero-rated** supplies under **CAD 30,000** over the four previous calendar quarters. Registration is required even if all supplies are zero-rated. — [Mondaq, "Charging GST/HST on digital marketing income… AdSense… zero-rating risks"](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/canada/sales-taxes-vat-gst/1778564/charging-gsthst-on-digital-marketing-income-in-canada-adsense-nfts-and-zero-rating-risks), [uWaterloo tax tips](https://uwaterloo.ca/school-of-accounting-and-finance/catalogs/tax-tips-university-students/gsthst-registration-and-filing-requirements-individuals)
- AdSense revenue paid by Google, a non-resident, is "generally" treated as a zero-rated supply. One law firm warns that zero-rating is **not automatic**. The relevant provision is Schedule VI, Part V, section 8: an advertising service supplied to a non-resident who is **not registered** for GST/HST. — [Mondaq (law firm)](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/canada/sales-taxes-vat-gst/1778564/charging-gsthst-on-digital-marketing-income-in-canada-adsense-nfts-and-zero-rating-risks), [salaccounting 2025](https://salaccounting.ca/blog/canadian-youtuber-tax-guide/)
- Sponsorships from **Canadian** companies are taxable supplies (GST/HST charged), not zero-rated. Voluntary registration lets you recover input tax credits on expenses. — [gondaliyacpa](https://gondaliyacpa.ca/?p=36407), [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/canada/sales-taxes-vat-gst/1778564/charging-gsthst-on-digital-marketing-income-in-canada-adsense-nfts-and-zero-rating-risks)

### Inferences
- **Which rate applies to Canada (inference, VERIFY with accountant):** Google classes YPP as "Other copyright royalties". Under Article XII(3), cultural copyright royalties are exempt (0%) unless they are "in connection with television broadcasting", and the residual rate is 10%. YouTube is not television broadcasting, which explains why most Canadians report 0%. The worst case is **10%**, not 15% or 30%, provided the W-8BEN is filed correctly. Any withholding is creditable against Canadian tax, so the net cost is mostly cash flow. Check the rate shown in AdSense after submitting the form.
- Order of magnitude: on $10,000 of YPP revenue with 80% from US viewers, the gap between 0% and 10% is about $800 per year. Leaving the form unfiled would cost $2,400 (24% of worldwide earnings).
- Canadian income tax (general knowledge, not verified by a primary source this session, VERIFY): income is reported as **business income** (form T2125). As a sole proprietor you pay both the employee and employer shares of CPP on net self-employment income. Expenses can be deducted (AI tools, software, a share of equipment).
- GST/HST: at the year-12 revenue levels modelled below (under CAD 30k), the creator would probably stay a small supplier in the first year. Once total revenue, **including zero-rated AdSense**, passes CAD 30k over four quarters, registration becomes mandatory. Canadian affiliate commissions (Wealthsimple, etc.), Canadian sponsors and sales of a digital product to Canadians would then be taxable. Voluntary registration early on can pay off if expenses carry GST/HST (software subscriptions billed in Canada).
- YPP itself has no Canadian-specific restriction in the sources read. Canada is a standard YPP and AdSense market (inference; see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No official Google or IRS document that **names the rate applied to Canada**. 0% versus 10% is still unresolved.
- AdSense payment terms for Canada (payment in CAD by EFT, CAD 100 threshold) were not verified in this session.
- No CRA primary page fetched (the small-supplier threshold and zero-rating are confirmed only by law firms and accountants). No CRA ruling found on whether Google is "registered for GST/HST", which affects zero-rating.
- No 2026 change specific to Canadian creators found.

## 3. Compliance: FTC, Ad Standards / Competition Bureau, FINRA, CSA/CIRO, YouTube policies

### Takeaway
A Canadian creator with a US-majority audience is covered by **both sets of rules**. US viewers bring in the **FTC** (clear disclosure inside the video), plus **FINRA** indirectly through the broker partner, which must review and archive content. Canada brings **Ad Standards** (updated guidelines of **2025-10-08**, with "#Ad" preferred and explicit coverage of affiliate links and **AI**), the **Competition Act**, and above all **CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-369 (2025-12-11)** on finfluencers. Under that notice, specific recommendations on securities can trigger a **registration obligation**, and **disclaimers do not remove it**. A "story" or educational channel that recommends no specific security, discloses every link, and makes no return promises keeps the risk low.

### Cited findings
- **Ad Standards (Canada), Influencer Marketing Disclosure Guidelines, edition of 2025-10-08:** new guidance on AI (disclosure rules apply even to AI-generated influencers), **affiliate marketing**, and child audiences. **#Ad** is the preferred disclosure. A platform tool is enough only if it is "prominent, clear and effective". When in doubt, add your own disclosure. Ad Standards cannot fine, but it can require members to amend or remove content. — [Ad Standards PDF 2025-10-08](https://adstandards.ca/wp-content/uploads/Ad-Standards-Influencer-Marketing-Disclosure-Guidelines-2025-10-08.pdf), [Gowling WLG 2025](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2025/updated-influencer-marketing-disclosure-guidelines-in-canada), [Ad Standards program](https://adstandards.ca/programs/influencer-marketing/)
- A Canadian Code of Advertising Standards for influencer marketing also applies to AI-generated content (December 2025 analysis). — [Bennett Jones, Dec. 2025](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2025/12/Canadian-Code-of-Advertising-Standards-for-Influencer-Marketing), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/social-media/1718226/canadian-code-of-advertising-standards-for-influencer-marketing-key-clauses-and-application-to-ai-generated-content-and-influencers)
- **Competition Bureau:** the reference documents are "Deceptive Marketing Practices Digest, Vol. 4" and "Influencer marketing and the Competition Act" (cited by Ad Standards). — [Ad Standards PDF](https://adstandards.ca/wp-content/uploads/Ad-Standards-Influencer-Marketing-Disclosure-Guidelines-2025-10-08.pdf)
- **CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-369 (2025-12-11), finfluencers:** opinions on specific securities or investment recommendations can trigger registration and compliance obligations **regardless of format or platform**. **Disclaimers alone do not remove these obligations.** Compensation received directly or indirectly from an issuer or registrant must be disclosed. The notice also guides firms (dealers) that work with finfluencers. — [BCSC, 31-369](https://www.bcsc.bc.ca/securities-law/law-and-policy/instruments-and-policies/3-registration-requirements-related-matters/current/31-369/), [CIRO news](https://www.ciro.ca/node/31746), [NSSC PDF](https://nssc.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/docs/2025-12-11%20CSA%20NR_%20Finfluencers_Guidance_VF_EN.pdf), [Mondaq analysis](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/securities/1761766/csa-guidance-on-finfluencers-compliance-considerations-for-registrants-and-issuers-engaging-content-creators)
- CIRO opened a review of online investment advice on 2025-12-17. — [Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/news/canadian-investment-regulatory-organization-launches-review-to-improve-access-to-20251217)
- **FTC (US):** disclosure must be "clear and conspicuous" and inside the content itself. YouTube's paid-promotion label may not be enough. — see `economie_niche.md` ([Traverse Legal](https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/ftc-influencer-guidelines/)).
- **FINRA:** M1 Finance was fined $850,000 in March 2024 for unsupervised influencer content paid per new customer (Rule 2210). Cobra Trading was fined $200,000. — see `economie_niche.md` ([Fortune 2024-03-19](https://fortune.com/2024/03/19/m1-finance-finra-sec-enforcement-fine-social-media-influencers)).
- **YouTube:** the Spam policy bans promoting "get rich quick" investment schemes. The Community Guidelines ban content meant to "scam, mislead, spam, or defraud". The External links policy bans links to phishing pages that collect financial information. YPP penalizes "financially abusive behaviours" (exclusion or termination). Monetization also requires following the advertiser-friendly content guidelines. — [Spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en), [Community Guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9288567?hl=en), [External links policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9054257?hl=en), [YPP monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en), [Creator responsibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7650329?hl=en)

### Inferences (practical checklist for a "story-format" finance channel)
1. **Spoken + on-screen disclosure** at the start of any video with an affiliate or sponsor ("This video contains affiliate links; I may earn a commission"), **#Ad** in the description, YouTube's paid-promotion box checked. This satisfies the FTC and Ad Standards together.
2. **No buy/sell recommendation on a named security**, no price targets, no "you'll make X%". This is the main CSA 31-369 trigger, and a disclaimer does not cure it. The story format ("how X went bankrupt", "why your credit card costs you…") is naturally on the safe side.
3. **Broker links**: expect the broker to require **pre-approval of scripts** (FINRA). That slows automation, so place broker links on evergreen videos approved once.
4. **"Not financial advice / educational purposes only"**: useful for viewers, but legally insufficient on its own (CSA 31-369). Keep it, without relying on it.
5. **AI voice/images**: Ad Standards (Oct. 2025) applies its rules to AI content. YouTube also requires synthetic-content labelling for realistic altered content (general knowledge, not verified this session).
6. No "get rich quick" thumbnails or titles (YouTube Spam policy). Advertisers and YouTube penalize sensational promises.

### Gaps
- The finance-specific provisions of the advertiser-friendly guidelines were not read.
- The exact text of the Competition Bureau's documents was not fetched.
- Whether CSA 31-369 applies to a Canadian resident whose audience is **mostly US** (territorial reach): no source. VERIFY with a securities lawyer if the channel ever names specific securities.
- YouTube's policy on "inauthentic/mass-produced" content for AI faceless channels (renamed in 2025): not verified this session. Important for the channel format, see the production researcher's notes.

## 4. Revenue model, months 0–12 (launch November 2026)

### Takeaway
Under the 2027 rules, **ads bring in nothing before roughly months 7–12 even in a good scenario**, and nothing at all in the frequent scenario where a channel stalls. **Affiliate links pay from the first view** and are the only income during the 8,000-hour phase. A small digital product and the first sponsors (1k–10k subscribers) can match or beat ads in year 1. Realistic cumulative range over 12 months (inference, see assumptions): **about $0–$500** (stalled channel), **about $2k–$6k** (median-good), **about $8k–$25k** (strong) for scenario (c).

### Cited findings (inputs)
- YPP entry for a new channel: 1,000 subscribers + 8,000 qualified hours (365 days) from 2027-02-01. — [YouTube Help 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- Planning RPM: $8–$15 for personal finance / investing, $5–$10 for business docs (see `economie_niche.md`, OutlierKit / Lenostube, no methodology).
- **Affiliate CTR**: a long-form personal finance video gets roughly **0.3%–1.2% of views clicking a link** (≥1.5% on a strong match). Another agency gives 0.6%–1.5%. That is about **3–15 clicks per 1,000 views**. — [CreatorsAgency, personal finance benchmarks 2026](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/youtube-creator-performance-benchmarks-personal-finance-2026), [CreatorsAgency, finance sponsorship benchmarks 2026](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/youtube-sponsorship-benchmarks-finance-niche-2026)
- Average affiliate conversion 3.2% (4.1% for review videos). Finance affiliate earnings about **$6.40 per 1,000 views**. Pinned comment CTR 6.3% versus 3.8% for the description (not finance-specific, no sample size). — [zebracat, YouTube affiliate statistics](https://www.zebracat.ai/post/youtube-affiliate-marketing-statistics) (vendor, low reliability)
- First sponsorships at 1k–10k subscribers: **$50–$500 per video**. Established finance sponsors pay $25–$80 CPM (see `economie_niche.md`). — [LearningRevolution 2026](https://www.learningrevolution.net/youtube-sponsorships/)

### Inferences: model (all trajectories are ASSUMPTIONS, not data)
**Common assumptions**: 1 long-form video of 12–15 min per week. Average view about 5 min, so **8,000 h ≈ 96,000 long-form views**. 1 subscriber per 100–200 views, so 1,000 subscribers ≈ 100k–200k views. RPM **$10** (personal finance/investing, US-majority audience, 0% withholding assumed). About 1 month of YPP review after the threshold. M1 = November 2026, so M12 = October 2027 (the Q4 2027 boost falls outside the window, while the Q2 tax-season boost is inside).

Monthly long-form view trajectories (assumptions):
| Month | Stalled | Median-good | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1–M3 | 0.3k–1k/mo | 1k → 5k | 2k → 10k |

**(a) Ads only** (RPM $10):
- Stalled: $0 (YPP not reached).
- Median-good: M10–M12 ≈ $450 + $550 + $650 ≈ **$1,500–$2,000** total for the year.
- Strong: M8–M12 ≈ $600 → $1,300/month, so **≈ $4,500–$5,000** total for the year.

**(b) Ads + affiliate**: affiliate revenue per 1,000 views = clicks (3–12) × conversion (1–5%) × payout ($20–$60, a mix of subscriptions like Monarch $20 / TradingView / Seeking Alpha and the occasional broker or Wealthsimple account). That gives about **$1–$15 per 1,000 views**, with **$2–$8** as the planning range (consistent with the $6.40 vendor figure). It starts in M1, with no threshold.
- Stalled: 25k views × $2–8 ≈ **$50–$200**.
- Median-good: 300k × $2–8 ≈ $600–$2,400 from affiliates, so **≈ $2,100–$4,400** with ads.
- Strong: 600k × $2–8 ≈ $1,200–$4,800 from affiliates, so **≈ $5,700–$9,800** with ads.

**(c) Ads + affiliate + sponsors + PDF workbook ($15–$29)**:
- Sponsors: from about 1,000–5,000 subscribers. Median-good ≈ 1–3 deals at $100–$500 in M9–M12, so **$200–$1,500**. Strong ≈ 4–8 deals at $300–$1,500 (CPM $30–$40 on 20–40k views per video), so **$2,000–$8,000**.
- Workbook (ASSUMPTION with no source: 0.02–0.1% of views buy, at $19): median-good 300k → **$1,100–$5,700**; strong 600k → **$2,300–$11,400**. Stalled ≈ $100–$500.
- **Total (c)**: stalled ≈ $150–$700. Median-good ≈ **$3,400–$11,600**. Strong ≈ **$10,000–$29,000**. The conservative lower half of each range is the most likely.

**Reading against the user's priorities**:
- *Speed*: affiliate links (day 1), then a digital product (as soon as there is an audience), then Creator Partnerships / fan funding (500 subscribers + 3,000 h, possibly around M5–M8), then ads (8,000 h).
- *Revenue at 12 months*: in the median scenarios, affiliate + product ≥ ads in year 1.
- *Automation*: subscription affiliates (Monarch, TradingView, Seeking Alpha) and the PDF are "set and forget". Brokers (FINRA script review) and sponsors (negotiation, custom script) are the least automatable.
- Late 2026 hours count toward an application after February 2027, but reaching **4,000 h before 2027-02-01** to get in under the old rule is unrealistic for a channel launched in November 2026 (it would take about 48k views in under 3 months).

## 5. Best sub-niche (RPM × affiliate accessibility for a Canadian creator)

### Takeaway
Inference: the **"investing basics / personal finance told as stories"** angle offers the best compromise (RPM about $10–$20). Its natural affiliates are tools and subscriptions open to non-US publishers (TradingView, Seeking Alpha, Monarch, Rocket Money) plus Wealthsimple for Canadians, and its compliance risk is moderate if no security is recommended. **Credit / credit cards** has the highest RPM ($16–$26) but the least accessible affiliate path for a small Canadian publisher. **Taxes** is tied to one country (the US tax system), which is awkward for a Canadian to cover credibly, and seasonal. **Business stories** carry little risk and appeal broadly, but have a lower RPM ($5–$14) and affiliate links that fit less naturally.

- RPM by sub-niche: credit cards $16–26, investing $13–21, taxes $13–19, retirement $12–18, personal finance $11–19, debt $10–16; business docs $5–14. — [OutlierKit 2026](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche) (via `economie_niche.md`, no methodology)
