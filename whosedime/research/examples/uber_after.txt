"In 2008, on a snowy night in Paris, Garrett Camp and I had trouble getting a cab." That's how Travis Kalanick tells it. Two friends, one cold street, no taxi. What they built next became Uber: you tap your phone, a black car finds you, and the fare goes on your card before you even step out. But for years, that ride was strangely cheap. Cheaper than it had any right to be. A transport consultant went through the numbers Uber showed its own investors. The estimate: riders were paying about 41 percent of what each trip really cost. So who covered the rest? Not you. Somebody else paid the gap, ride after ride, city after city. Uber only turned a profit from its own business, over a full year, in 2023. This is the story of who paid for your cheap ride.
