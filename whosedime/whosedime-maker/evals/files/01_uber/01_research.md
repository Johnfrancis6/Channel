# Research: 01_uber, "Uber Was Cheap. Who Paid?" (2026-11-07, S04 eval run)

**The story in one sentence**: two friends wanted a ride at the tap of a
button; for years the ride cost riders less than it cost to make, and the
gap was paid by investors, then by drivers, then by New York's taxi owners.
**Payer chain**: investors (F05-F07, F13-F14) → drivers (F09-F10, F19-F21)
→ taxi medallion owners (F15-F17). Same order as the PLAN_ACTION lead;
riders are not a payer here (they were the ones paying less).

## The decision

June 1, 2016: Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund puts $3.5 billion into
Uber at a $62.5 billion valuation; Yasir Al Rumayyan joins the board
(F07, F08). The decision to keep paying for cheap rides. **Gap**: the
earlier decision that made rides cheap (the pricing of the low-cost
service) is not sourced here; see "Leads not used".

## Follow the dollar (3 steps)

1. **Investors**: losses of $2 billion on $1.4 billion of revenue in the
   year to September 2015 (F06); $7.9 billion of accumulated deficit by
   the end of 2018 (F13); IPO in May 2019, closed below its price (F14).
2. **Drivers**: promised more than $90,000 in New York, median more like
   $61,000 (F10); $20 million settlement (F09); 20% of each fare kept by
   Uber in the London cases (F19).
3. **Taxi medallion owners**: about $1 million a medallion before Uber and
   Lyft (F15); crash in late 2014, more than 950 bankruptcies (F16);
   15-day hunger strike in 2021 (F17).

## Three scenes (person, place, gesture, line)

1. **Travis Kalanick and Garrett Camp, Paris, 2008**: a snowy night, no
   cab. Line: "Why can't we just tap a button and get a ride?" (Camp's
   idea in Kalanick's words; F01, F02).
2. **Dara Khosrowshahi, the IPO, May 2019**: the shares close below their
   price. Line: "It was a tough week to go public, but we got it done." (F14)
3. **Mr Aslam and Mr Farrar, London, 2021**: two drivers win at the
   Supreme Court. Line (the court): "the only way in which they can
   increase their earnings is by working longer hours" (F18). No gesture
   is sourced: the narrator tells the ruling, not the room.

## Nuance (the "but")

- The 41% is **one analyst's estimate** from figures Uber gave investors,
  not audited accounts (F05).
- Uber **did not admit** the FTC's allegations (F09).
- The medallion crash has **its own causes** (the loans); Uber and Lyft
  are named as the turning point by PoliticsNY (F15), not as the only cause.

## Consequences

UK ruling (F18-F22); 2022 loss, 2023 first full year of operating profit
(F23, F24); nearly 26 million trips a day (F25).
